A few stand out especially.

REFUSE
→ already takes a posture against one possible move

Even “refuse to define weighted-word first” is not neutral. It directs the inquiry away from definition.

PLACE
→ positions the reader somewhere

“Place the reader inside the conditions” is almost architectural. It does not merely invite; it situates.

GOVERN
→ carries jurisdiction

“The axioms could govern the inquiry” may be one of the heaviest phrases in the whole passage, because it gives the axioms more than orienting presence.

Then the three explanations themselves are highly directional:

do not force
do not collapse
do not assume

Those are restraints, but they are still imperatives.

So there is an interesting tension:

NON-COERCION
expressed as
DO NOT FORCE

NON-RESOLUTION
expressed as
DO NOT COLLAPSE

NON-CLOSURE
expressed as
DO NOT ASSUME

The posture resists coercion through language that itself directs behavior.

That does not make it wrong. It makes it worth hearing.

Then there is a second family:

EXTRACTED
COMPRESSED
DISPLACED
TRAVEL
ARRIVE
EDGE

These all imply motion, transfer, pressure, or boundary.

They give the inquiry a physical grammar:

word
→ extracted

alternatives
→ compressed

weight
→ travels

cost
→ displaced

inquiry
→ arrives

pamphlet
→ has an edge

That is quite a lot of directional structure for a passage trying not to predetermine relation.

And one phrase now stands out to me more than it did when I first wrote it:

> “the pamphlet's natural open edge”



NATURAL
→ may imply that this ending is somehow proper or inherent

OPEN
→ appears non-closing

EDGE
→ still establishes a boundary

So even the phrase meant to preserve non-closure may quietly suggest where the pamphlet ought to end.

That feels very close to The Weighted Phrase itself.

The passage may already be demonstrating weighted-word:

The most interesting words to me, if we were going to inspect only a very small set, would be:

because each seems to do more than report an idea. Each may be conducting the inquiry in some direction.

And that makes me wonder whether the pamphlet's first specimen should perhaps be its own language.
